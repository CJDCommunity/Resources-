🚀 Google Java Backend Engineer Interview Experience | 4 Rounds | DSA, Core Java & System Design  
  
What separates an average interview performance from a strong one? It’s not just knowing the answers — it’s understanding the why behind every technical decision.  
  
Here’s a breakdown of the interview rounds for a Java Backend Engineer role at Google. 👇  
  
🔹 Round 1: DSA & Coding  
  
* Trapping Rain Water using an optimized two-pointer approach  
* Top K Frequent Elements using a Heap/Priority Queue  
* Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters  
* Explain the time and space complexity of your solutions  
  
🔹 Round 2: Core Java & Concurrency  
  
* How does HashMap work internally, and why isn’t it thread-safe?  
* What happens when a thread pool’s queue becomes full?  
* Which thread pool does CompletableFuture use by default?  
* How do you detect and resolve a deadlock using a thread dump?  
* How would you investigate an OutOfMemoryError in production?  
  
🔹 Round 3: System Design  
  
* Design a payment system capable of handling millions of transactions  
* Prevent duplicate payments using idempotency  
* Handle failures when a debit succeeds but a credit fails using the Saga pattern  
* Maintain event ordering with Kafka partitions  
* Prevent cascading failures using circuit breakers, retries, and timeouts  
* Explain how you would scale the system by 10x  
  
🔹 Round 4: Project Deep Dive & Behavioral  
  
* Explain your most complex project end to end  
* Why did you choose your design over alternative approaches?  
* Describe a production incident you handled and what you learned  
* How do you handle disagreements over technical decisions?  
  
💡 Key Takeaways for Java Developers  
  
✅ Explain your approach before writing code.  
  
✅ Discuss trade-offs, not just the final solution.  
  
✅ Connect technical concepts to real-world project scenarios.  
  
✅ Understand time and space complexity.  
  
✅ Stay calm when interviewers challenge your approach.  
  
🔥 The biggest lesson: Senior backend interviews test your problem-solving process, engineering judgment, and ability to explain decisions — not just your knowledge of definitions.
